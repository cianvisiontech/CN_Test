<p>To turn social media growth into e-commerce sales, a brand needs a clear brand
									strategy that gives shoppers a reason to choose its product, a checkout that shows
									total costs early and asks for little effort, ways to reduce cash on delivery
									returns, and reports that count paid, delivered orders instead of followers. Social
									media marketing reports can lead with followers and reach, but revenue depends on
									what happens after the follow, and the data below shows where shoppers drop off.</p>
								<br>
								<h2>Why Do Followers Rarely Turn Into Customers?</h2>
								<p><a style="color: blue;"
										href="https://www.rivaliq.com/blog/social-media-industry-benchmark-report/"
										target="_blank">Rival IQ’s report</a>, which covered 2,100 companies, found a
									median Instagram engagement
									rate of 0.47 percent per post, measured against followers. At that rate, a brand
									with 100,000 followers would see about 470 of them interact with a typical post.</p>
								<p>The place where purchases happen also keeps changing. <a style="color: blue;"
										href="https://www.shopifreaks.com/meta-to-phase-out-onsite-checkout-for-facebook-and-instagram-shops/"
										target="_blank">Shopifreaks</a> reported that Meta
									began moving Facebook and Instagram Shops to website checkout in June 2025, and that
									most merchants would have to send customers to their own websites to complete
									purchases by the end of August. This reversed a 2023 Meta mandate that required
									onsite checkout. For most merchants, social media marketing can start the journey,
									but the seller’s own store finishes it.</p> <br>
								<h2>What Stops Shoppers From Paying?</h2>
								<p><a style="color: blue;" href="https://baymard.com/lists/cart-abandonment-rate"
										target="_blank">Baymard Institute</a> calculates an average cart abandonment
									rate of 70.22 percent across
									50 different studies. Its survey found 42 percent of US online shoppers had
									abandoned a cart because they were just browsing, so some abandonment is
									unavoidable. Setting browsers aside, extra costs such as shipping, tax and fees were
									the top reason at 40 percent, 17 percent cited a long or complicated checkout, 12
									percent could not see the total order cost upfront, and 19 percent did not trust the
									site with their credit card information.</p>
								<p>Baymard also found that the average US checkout shows 23.48 form elements, while its
									testing shows an ideal flow can use 12 to 14. It estimates that better checkout
									design could lift conversion by 35.26 percent for the average large ecommerce site,
									and for US and EU ecommerce combined it puts recoverable lost orders at $260
									billion.</p> <br>
								<h2>Why Does Cash on Delivery Hurt Indian Sellers?</h2>
								<p>At a 2024 TechSparks panel reported by <a style="color: blue;"
										href="https://yourstory.com/2024/03/d2c-expert-strategies-reducing-returns"
										target="_blank">YourStory</a>, Delhivery’s head of product said
									roughly 60 percent of ecommerce orders are cash on delivery (COD). Sanil Jain of The
									Better Shop said return to origin (RTO) rates on COD orders are nearly five times
									higher than on prepaid ones, averaging around 15 to 20 percent.</p>
								<p>Payments company <a style="color: blue;"
										href="https://www.cashfree.com/blog/turning-cod-from-a-margin-leak-into-a-growth-lever-for-d2c-with-one-click-checkout/"
										target="_blank">Cashfree</a>, which sells checkout tools, says removing COD
									completely
									lowers conversions, and reports that only about 75 percent of COD orders shipped
									across its brands are delivered. Its study found that 71 percent of customers want
									to see the product before paying, and 67 percent worry about fraud or payment
									failures. For social media marketing aimed at Indian shoppers, COD risk belongs in
									the plan from the start.</p> <br>
								<h2>How Do You Turn Followers Into Buyers?</h2>
								<p>Each step below ties social media marketing to a sale a business can actually count.
								</p>
								<h4>Start with brand strategy</h4>
								<p>Brand strategy means writing down who the product is for, what it does better than
									the alternatives and what proof backs that up. A brand strategy gives every post,
									product page and package the same answer to one question: why should a stranger buy
									this?</p>
								<h4>Learn how to differentiate your product from competitors</h4>
								<p>Inside your brand strategy, how to differentiate your product from competitors comes
									down to choosing one difference a shopper can check quickly, such as a visible
									result, the founder’s reason for making the product, a specific ingredient or a
									guarantee, and repeating it on every post and product page.</p>
								<h4>Fix the checkout before buying more traffic</h4>
								<p>Show the total price and delivery cost early, trim the checkout form, and avoid
									forcing account creation, which 18 percent of surveyed shoppers cited as a reason
									for abandoning a cart.</p>
								<h4>Make prepaid the easier choice</h4>
								<p>Plum Goodness said a minimal COD handling charge brought its return rates down from
									40 to 45 percent to about 20 percent. Delhivery’s Nikhil Vij called COD convenience
									fees and verifying intent before shipping essential interventions, and added that
									even a one-day delivery delay can lead to a spike in returns.</p>
								<h4>Measure paid and delivered orders</h4>
								<p>Track cost per purchase, repeat purchase rate, revenue per visitor and
									delivered-order rate by content type and platform, so each post in a social media
									marketing plan is judged by the orders it produces instead of the reach it earns.
								</p> <br>
								<h2>What Does Good Social Media Marketing Look Like?</h2>
								<p>Good social media marketing connects attention, trust and checkout into one journey,
									backed by a brand strategy and owned by someone from first view to delivered parcel.
									A simple test is to order from your own store on a phone and note every moment you
									hesitate or doubt.</p> <br>
